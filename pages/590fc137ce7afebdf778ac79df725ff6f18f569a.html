Sousou no Frieren
add
Both works flirt with the *Iyashikei* genre (stories that heal or comfort the soul). They feature beautiful landscapes, soothing soundtracks, and a sense of peace—though one always accompanied by a tinge of sadness (the inevitability of saying goodbye). The focus isn't necessarily on constant epic battles or grand conflicts. The narrative intentionally lingers on everyday moments, casual conversations, and simple rituals. In both *Frieren* and *Summer Pockets*, the core message is that small, everyday memories are the most precious when we look back. If you appreciated the slow pace, the focus on feelings, and the emotional sensitivity of *Frieren*, you are very likely to be moved by—and form a strong connection with—the story of *Summer Pockets*

Cherry no Manma and Ano Ko ni 1000% were released as ten-minute bonuses at the end of the first and second LD/VHS of Umi no Yami, Tsuki no Kage, respectively. Both projects were based on shoujo romance manga and produced by the same studio, and the teams that worked on them were almost identical. The main strength of the both anime is that very shoujo style of the turn of the 80s and 90s: in technical terms, in terms of plot, and in terms of characters and tropes. There is no clear and fixed beginning, no definitive closure. Just riding on the feel train without stations of departure and arrival. The feel train and no boundaries. A small sip of feelings that helps you breathe.
