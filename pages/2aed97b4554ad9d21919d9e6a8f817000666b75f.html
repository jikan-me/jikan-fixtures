Spoiler
I have completed the second season of Saga of Tanya the Evil and I gave it a 7/10

It’s been a while, but The Saga of Tanya the Evil is finally back with a brand-new season. After rewatching the first season as a refresher, I came away from this one feeling both a little distracted and genuinely interested at the same time. At first glance that sounds contradictory, but it actually makes a lot of sense.

What I considered the biggest flaw of the first season has become this season’s greatest strength, yet it also creates a new problem: Tanya now feels like a side character in ... her own anime. She is, quite literally, a soldier carrying out orders from her superiors. This season is all about the bigger picture. War and politics take center stage, and Tanya mainly serves as a lens through which we see different aspects of the conflict, whether it’s life on the home front, the planning of offensives, or the morale of the enemy.

The real focus lies with the Empire’s generals, Hans von Zettour and Kurt von Rudersdorf, as they search for an exit strategy to bring the war to an end. Along the way they have to deal with the government’s objectives, the struggle on the eastern front, and the neutral nation of Ildoa (the in-world counterpart of the Kingdom of Italy).

I really enjoy the contrast between the fictional universe and how closely it mirrors real history between 1914 and 1945. That’s one of the anime’s biggest strengths, alongside its excellent animation and soundtrack. We see far less of Being X (God) this time around; he only shows up briefly at the end, but it makes for a pretty good cliffhanger.

The other thing that kept me interested is the season’s central theme: how to end a war, and how differently that question looks from a military perspective versus a political one. Ending a war is easy. Negotiating favorable terms is the hard part, especially when it means giving up territory or paying reparations. It made me think of today’s Russia–Ukraine war. For Russia, ending the war is extremely difficult, because the humiliation of a bad deal is arguably the worst outcome, so it seeks a major victory as its exit strategy. That’s exactly what the Empire does in this season.

Overall, this is a solid continuation of The Saga of Tanya the Evil. Its different approach brings something fresh, but if the story is going to improve, it needs to bring the focus back to Tanya herself.

I DON'T NEED TO SEE THE PANTY COLLECTION OF EVERY SINGLE FEMALE CHARACTER IN THE ENTIRE CAST TO APPRECIATE A WORK OF FICTION!

And that's how this review begins, because I genuinely don't understand... Why turn a zombie apocalypse survival series into an ECCHI?!

I'm not entirely against this genre, even though it doesn't interest me, but some works simply don't need it... It's just counterproductive. It undermines the seriousness of the story and the sense of gravity surrounding the events. The female characters are degraded, constantly being shown in their panties and bent over as far as physically possible. How am I supposed to take these ... characters seriously when they actually had some potential?

Honestly, the first five episodes showed promise. Sure, there were already a few questionable moments, with panties here and there and Shizuka's assets going "Boing Boing" with every step she takes, but it was manageable. The main characters seemed interesting: Saeko, Takashi, Rei, Kota, Saya... They all seemed to have hidden depths that could gradually be explored as the story progressed.

Then we get to episode 6, and that's where everything goes downhill...

An almost entire episode where you get to enjoy the four main female characters taking an uncensored bath together while getting drunk, only to then start making advances toward the two main male characters. JUST WHY?!

And then comes episode 7, where the female characters obviously haven't had time to get dressed again, so they spend the entire episode in their panties. What a coincidence!

I understand that for some aspiring authors, making ecchi or hentai content can be an easier way to sell their work. But doing this in a series like this one, which had the potential to feature interesting, well-developed characters and a promising story and plot on paper, is just a waste. Even if I do think the story drags on for too long and takes far too much time to actually reveal anything meaningful to us. (Then again, when you dedicate an entire episode to showing all your female characters naked, you're not exactly helping your own case...)

Of course, I'd vaguely heard of Highschool of the Dead before watching it, mainly because of that infamous scene where Takashi uses Rei's breasts to help him aim his sniper rifle. Honestly, I expected to laugh when I finally saw that scene in context. But the problem is that the series has gone SO overboard with the sexualization of its female characters that, by that point, it just disgusted me even more. My only reaction was basically, "Here we go again with another pointless scene..."

Because that's the real problem: showing some of a character's intimate side isn't ALWAYS a bad thing. It can help bring complicated romances to a conclusion or sometimes serve a comedic purpose, particularly in lighter series.

But in a work like this one, which isn't trying to be a comedy, doesn't develop any romantic relationships, and aims to be gory, tragic, and occasionally dark, sexualizing its characters serves NO PURPOSE WHATSOEVER. It adds absolutely nothing to the story, other than giving horny teenagers the chance to fantasize about being in Takashi's shoes.

In my opinion, this series is a massive waste of potential. That being said, I do want to give credit to the animation, which is genuinely impressive for a 2010 production, with some remarkably ambitious shots for its time. The action scenes are still pretty engaging, and that's what kept me hooked during the first five episodes. I also appreciate the zombie outbreak premise, which isn't just a copy of The Walking Dead, as some people claim. After all, the manga dates back to 2006...

You'll like it if:
• You enjoy traveller adventures with a bit of mystery.
• You like dark and twisted themes wrapped in an otherwise cute-looking anime.
• You enjoy watching a group of misfits come together, help each other, and embark on a quest of self-discovery.
• You appreciate atmospheric environments.

You won't like it if:
• You have a strong aversion to lolicon or anything associated with it.
• You have a strong aversion to what can sometimes only be described as torture p*.
• You prefer a large main cast with complex, intertwined relationships.
... • Dark and twisted shows really aren't your thing.

Story: Made in Abyss Season 1 is essentially the story of two kids embarking on a journey into, well, the Abyss. Along the way, we learn more about the secrets hidden within its different levels, as well as the pasts and origins of our main characters. Personally, I really liked the build-up to the journey, and I thoroughly enjoyed the almost game-level-like exploration of the Abyss, with all the weird environments, creatures, and individuals each level has to offer. The twists get progressively darker, and certain moments are particularly intense, especially considering the ages of the characters. Some scenes and dialogues are also surprisingly explicit given their ages, without really contributing much to the plot, which is... a choice.

Characters: Our main characters are Riko and Reg, so if you don't like them for whatever reason, you're really out of luck, since they remain the main focus for most of the season. Or maybe you're in luck, because you'll get to see them suffer for most of it (?) Hopefully, you're not that kind of person. Regardless, I think they're pretty solid main characters, each with their own motivations, strengths, and flaws, all of which are explored adequately throughout the season. Their bond is also very cute. The supporting cast is interesting as well, with some really peculiar characters who help push the story forward. One character we get to know better later in the journey also receives some solid exploration and development.

Art & Sound: The environmental design is phenomenal. The colours and scenery are simply magical. Certain character designs are hard to translate from the manga to the anime, like Ozen's, but, overall, they did the best they could. The OST is also really strong, especially when "Hanezeve Caradhina" starts playing. I get chills. It's that good.

Personal enjoyment: Made in Abyss is a well-paced, visually stunning adventure with sympathetic characters, captivating mystery and a fascinating world to explore. That said, there are some aspects that give me the ick and prevent me from enjoying the show as much as I otherwise could have. These mostly come down to a few really random and unnecessary suggestive shots and dialogues, as well as moments of suffering and humiliation that made me question whether they were actually necessary to convey what the characters were feeling or whether they were, to put it the way people often do, the author's barely disguised fetish. So yeah, while I appreciate the show for what it offers as a dark adventure, I wouldn't wholeheartedly recommend it without this disclaimer. Some of its "creative choices" genuinely made me question my enjoyment of the show at times.
